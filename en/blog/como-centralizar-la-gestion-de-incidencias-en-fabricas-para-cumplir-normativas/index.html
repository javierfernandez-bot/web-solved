Centralizing Issue Management in Factories

By Claudia · November 28, 2025 · 9 min read

In industrial factories, issues happen every day: a production line stops, a batch shows a deviation, a machine needs urgent maintenance, or a quality non-conformity is detected.

In many plants, information is scattered across spreadsheets, emails, paper forms, and different systems (ERP, MES, CMMS). This leads to incomplete records, duplication, delays, and one critical consequence: difficulty meeting quality and safety regulations.

Meeting standards like ISO 9001, IFS, BRCGS, or ISO 22000 requires full traceability, clear evidence, and demonstrable response times. But when every department logs issues its own way, achieving that traceability becomes a monumental task.

In this article, we'll look at how to centralize issue management in a single system, the benefits of doing so, the main challenges, and how a platform like Solved can simplify the entire process so that regulatory compliance becomes a natural part of everyday work.

Problems With Decentralized Issue Management

A lack of centralization creates multiple challenges for companies:

1. Duplicate Data and Lost Information

When issues are managed across different systems (ERP, MES, CMMS, or even email and spreadsheets), data duplication is inevitable. The same incident can show up three times with a different description, owner, or status.
This creates confusion, errors in reports, and, in the worst case, decisions based on incorrect information.

2. Lack of Traceability

Quality standards require you to be able to answer a simple question:

"What happened, who logged it, what action was taken, and when was it resolved?"

In decentralized environments, this chain of traceability breaks down constantly. There's no single record, and auditors regularly find gaps in the documentation.

Result: non-conformities, observations, or penalties during audits.

3. Delays in Resolving Issues

When managers have to hunt for information across different channels, response times balloon.
Without a centralized system that automatically assigns tasks and notifies the right person, an issue can take days to resolve, affecting production or missed deadlines.

4. Direct Impact on Audits

Every audit turns into a race against the clock: gathering data, digging through spreadsheets, searching emails, validating evidence…
Decentralization multiplies the time spent on audits and increases the risk of errors or omissions. What should be a quick check turns into a stressful week of duplicated work.

What It Means to Centralize Issue Management

Centralizing issue management means bringing all the relevant information — logging, tracking, evidence, owners, and outcomes — together in a single, shared system accessible to every department involved: production, maintenance, quality, safety, environment, and so on.

The Key Components of Centralized Management

  1. Single issue log
     Each issue is documented once and classified by type, area, and priority.
  2. Defined owners and workflows
     The system automatically assigns tasks and owners, avoiding confusion or duplication.
  3. Digital evidence and full traceability
     Photos, videos, documents, and comments are linked to the case, creating a verifiable history.
  4. Integration with other systems (ERP, MES, CMMS)
     Information flows between platforms without manual data entry.
  5. Automatic alerts and notifications
     Every user gets real-time alerts about issues relevant to them, deadlines, or deviations.
  6. Real-time reporting
     Managers can see the overall status from a dashboard, without waiting for monthly reports.

In short: a single source of truth for every issue.

Benefits of Centralizing Issue Management

1. Greater Traceability and Control

Every issue has a complete history from detection to closure.
This makes it easier to comply with standards like ISO 9001, which require evidence of traceability in corrective and preventive actions (CAPA).
Having all the data centralized also enables trend analysis and failure-pattern detection.

2. Fewer Errors and Duplicates

With a single system, issues are only logged once.
This eliminates duplicates and ensures consistency across departments.
Data no longer depends on any one employee's memory or inbox.

3. Time Saved on Audits

With all the information digitized and traceable, preparing for an audit stops being a burden.
Auditors can access records, evidence, and actions directly, cutting review time by more than 70%.
Teams also gain confidence knowing everything is properly documented.

4. Easier Regulatory Compliance

The main quality and industrial safety standards require documented control of issues, response times, and corrective actions.
Centralizing your records makes it easier to comply with:

  • ISO 9001 – Quality management system
  • ISO 22000 – Food safety
  • IFS / BRCGS – Food safety and traceability
  • ISO 45001 – Occupational health and safety
  • ISO 14001 – Environment

With a centralized tool, evidence is generated automatically and preserved with no risk of loss or tampering.

5. Better Communication Between Departments

Centralizing also improves collaboration. Production, maintenance, and quality all work from the same real-time information.

Silos disappear and transparency increases, which speeds up decision-making and strengthens a culture of continuous improvement.

Challenges of Centralizing Management (and How to Overcome Them)

1. Team Resistance to Change

Some employees may be reluctant to give up spreadsheets or their traditional forms.

The key is to show tangible benefits from day one: less repetitive work, more visibility, and fewer errors.

Tip: start with a pilot in a small area and show measurable results (for example, a 30% reduction in average resolution time).

2. Migrating Historical Data

Moving information from spreadsheets or legacy systems can seem like an obstacle, but with the right approach it's entirely manageable.

The best approach is to define which data actually adds value, clean up duplicates, and migrate only what's necessary.

3. Integration With Existing Systems (ERP, MES, CMMS)

A centralized system needs to communicate smoothly with the rest of the plant's systems.

Modern platforms use APIs or standard connectors, which lets data sync automatically:

  • Issues detected on the plant floor are updated in the ERP.
  • Maintenance orders are also closed out in the CMMS.
  • Dashboards reflect changes in real time.

This avoids duplicate data entry and keeps every environment consistent.

4. Security and Access Control

Another critical aspect is making sure data is protected.

A centralized system should offer user profiles, change history, encryption, and automatic backups, ensuring compliance with GDPR and the organization's internal policies.
