Integrating Issue Management With Your ERP, MES, or CMMS

By Karim · October 7, 2025 · 6 min read

Issue management with an ERP is one of the biggest challenges for factories working with multiple digital systems. 

Issues or non-conformities often arise across different departments and need to be managed cross-functionally: quality, production, maintenance, logistics, and even purchasing or sales. 

The problem is that the tools already in place on the plant floor — ERP, MES, or CMMS — aren't designed to log those issues or provide full traceability for them. The result: duplication, slow processes, poor visibility, and wasted time that directly impacts quality and costs.

In this article, we'll look at why this problem happens, what role each system plays, the benefits of integrating issue management into your digital ecosystem, and how Solved offers a simpler, more efficient solution that doesn't require modifying your existing software.

What Data Duplication Means — and Why It Isn't the Main Problem

When we talk about data duplication in ERP issue management, we mean having to log the same issue on different platforms: for example, noting a batch defect in the ERP, documenting it again in the CMMS so maintenance can review it, and maybe once more in the MES so it shows up in production. This double or triple entry creates inconsistencies, errors, and unnecessary administrative work.

But the real problem isn't just duplication — it's the lack of cross-departmental reach and traceability.

  • The ERP is built to manage inventory, purchasing, billing, or logistics — not to follow an issue from the moment it occurs until it's resolved.
  • The MES controls production in real time, but its focus is the production process itself, not overall quality or corrective actions.
  • The CMMS manages asset maintenance, but leaves out quality issues, supplier problems, or audits.

This forces companies to build complex information flows that require modifying the software just to get systems talking to each other. In practice, few factories achieve a smooth integration, and in the meantime, they lose sight of what really matters: closing issues quickly and with full traceability.

The Systems Involved and Their Roles: ERP, MES, and CMMS

To better understand where ERP issue management falls short, let's look at the role each system plays:

  • ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning): the core of business management. Used for finance, purchasing, inventory, orders, and, in some cases, quality. However, it isn't designed to manage non-conformities with evidence, owner workflows, or real-time tracking.
  • MES (Manufacturing Execution System): controls and monitors production on the plant floor. It tracks line efficiency, work orders, or machine status. While it captures valuable data, its scope is limited to the production environment and it doesn't easily integrate supplier, customer, or audit issues.
  • CMMS (Computerized Maintenance Management System): focused on preventive and corrective asset maintenance. It's useful for technical issues, but leaves out quality problems, cross-functional processes, or documented non-conformities.

Ultimately, each system serves a specific purpose. The problem arises when an issue affects several departments and there's no common thread to follow it from start to finish.

Benefits of Integrating Issue Management Across ERP, MES, and CMMS

Integrating ERP issue management delivers clear benefits:

  • Full traceability: from the moment an issue is detected until it's resolved, everything is documented, regardless of the department.
  • Elimination of duplicate records: no more need to log the same thing across multiple systems.
  • Faster response times: issues go straight to the right owner, with no information lost along the way.
  • Better audit visibility: a single, verifiable history is available.
  • Operational optimization: less downtime, smoother processes, and continuous learning to avoid recurrences.

That said, integrating these systems is usually a challenge. It requires custom development, complex data flows, and an IT investment many factories aren't willing to make.

Common Challenges When Integrating Systems

  1. Technology differences: each piece of software uses different formats, APIs, and data structures.
  2. Change management: staff may resist new ways of logging or managing information.
  3. Data synchronization: real time or batch? Refresh time can be critical.
  4. Security and permissions: sharing data between systems demands rigorous management.
  5. Implementation costs: every integration is a project in its own right, with a risk of running over budget.

That's why many companies look for solutions that don't force the integration of every system, but instead act as a cross-cutting layer.

Steps for Effective Integration (Methodology)

  1. Internal audit of existing systems
     Map how issues are logged in ERP, MES, and CMMS. Identify duplicates and gaps.
  2. Define issue workflows and data mapping
     Decide what data needs to be shared between systems (owner, issue type, status, evidence).
  3. Choose an interface or integration method
     APIs, standard connectors, or middleware. Evaluate cost and complexity.
  4. Design a common data structure
     Standardize categories, priorities, and owners to avoid confusion.
  5. Run pilot tests
     Start in one specific area before rolling out across the whole factory.
  6. Train staff and define clear roles
     Make sure everyone knows where and how to log information.
  7. Monitor and adjust after implementation
     Track duplicate issues, resolution times, and unaddressed alerts.
