Complaint management that builds customer loyalty

In the agri-food sector, complaint management is much more than a mandatory administrative process: it's a real opportunity to show your company's commitment to quality and turn a negative experience into a stronger bond with the customer. Companies that handle complaints with speed, transparency and rigor don't just solve one-off problems — they build long-term trust. In this article we explain how to structure an effective complaint-handling system and how Solved helps you manage it from start to finish.

Why a well-handled complaint retains customers

Receiving a complaint is never pleasant, but ignoring it or handling it poorly costs far more. Consumer behavior studies repeatedly show that a customer whose complaint is resolved satisfactorily shows higher loyalty than one who never had a problem. Why? Effective conflict resolution sends a clear message: the company listens, takes responsibility and acts.

In the agri-food sector, this matters even more. Customer complaints can relate to product safety, foreign bodies, deviations in organoleptic characteristics, or labeling issues. Any of these cases can have regulatory as well as commercial implications. A fast, documented response protects the company legally and, at the same time, shows the customer that their concerns are taken seriously.

In addition, the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 of the European Parliament and of the Council (Regulation [EC] 178/2002, 2002) sets out the general principles of food law and underscores operators' responsibility for traceability and the management of incidents related to food safety. Having a formal complaint system isn't just good practice: in many contexts it's an implicit requirement of the European regulatory framework.

In short, well-handled complaints strengthen brand image, reduce customer loss and provide valuable insight for improving production processes.

The logging, analysis and response flow in complaint management

A robust complaint-handling system must cover at least three clearly defined phases: structured logging, root-cause analysis and the response to the customer. Improvising in any of these stages can lead to delays, inconsistent responses and, in the worst case, the same problem happening again.

Structured logging: Every complaint must be documented from the outset with precise information: date received, customer details, affected product, batch, a detailed description of the problem and the communication channel used. This initial record is the foundation the rest of the process is built on, and it allows complaints to be prioritized by severity or impact.

Root-cause analysis: Once the complaint is logged, the quality team must investigate the origin of the problem. Tools such as root-cause analysis (5 whys, Ishikawa diagram) help identify whether the failure occurred on the production line, during storage, in transport or in the raw material. This analysis doesn't just resolve the specific incident — it prevents it from happening again.

Response to the customer: The customer must receive a clear response, within a reasonable timeframe and with a level of detail suited to their profile. In sectors with industrial or distribution customers, the response usually includes a technical report with the corrective actions taken. In any case, communication must be honest and solution-oriented.

Setting indicators tracking indicators —such as the average time to close complaints or the recurrence rate— make it possible to evaluate the process's effectiveness and identify areas for continuous improvement.

Linking to non-conformities and suppliers

One of the most common mistakes in complaint management is treating complaints as isolated events, disconnected from the rest of the quality system. In reality, a customer complaint is often the visible symptom of an internal non-conformity or a problem in the supply chain.

That's why complaint handling must be integrated with the management system for incidents and non-conformities. When the root cause of a complaint is found to be a production defect, that non-conformity must be opened, investigated and closed with documented corrective actions. In this way, the complaint acts as the trigger for a real improvement in the process.

La vinculación con proveedores es igualmente crítica. Si la causa del problema es una materia prima defectuosa, un envase inadecuado o un ingrediente fuera de especificación, es necesario iniciar una reclamación formal al proveedor y, dependiendo de la gravedad, revisar su evaluación. La quality supplier audit gives you access to historical data on each supplier's performance, making it easier to make informed decisions: from a formal warning to switching to another approved supplier.

Standards such as BRCGS Global Standards (BRCGS, n.d.) specifically require this traceability between complaints, non-conformities and supplier actions as part of the quality management requirements. Having a digital system that connects all these elements isn't a luxury — it's a necessity for any company that wants to keep its certifications and stay competitive.
