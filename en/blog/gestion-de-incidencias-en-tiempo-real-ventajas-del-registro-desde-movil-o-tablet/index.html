Real-Time Issues from Phone or Tablet

By Karim · October 16, 2025 · 6 min read

Managing issues in real time is now a critical need for modern factories. 

For years, issue records were kept on paper or in hard-to-access systems, which caused delays in communication, incomplete data, and poor traceability. 

When an operator spotted a problem, they often had to wait until the end of the shift to report it, or rely on a supervisor to enter it into the system. The result was clear: issues reported late, errors piling up, and decisions coming too late.

Being able to log issues in real time straight from a phone or tablet has changed all that. Now factories can react quickly, cut down on errors, improve traceability, and make audits easier. In this article, we'll look at what it means to log issues in real time, its advantages, real use cases, technical requirements, common challenges, and how Solved makes this transformation possible.

What logging issues in real time means

Logging issues in real time means entering the details of a problem at the exact moment and place it happens. It's no longer about jotting something down on paper and entering it into a system later — it's about immediately documenting everything the owner needs, with context and digital evidence, so they can act without delay.

For this to work, three basic elements are needed:

  • Mobile devices or tablets that operators can use on the plant floor.
  • Reliable connectivity so data syncs instantly, though an offline option must still exist.
  • A dedicated app that lets you log issues, attach photos and videos, and send automatic notifications.

When these elements come together, managing issues in real time becomes a strategic tool for boosting factory efficiency.

Advantages of immediate logging

The advantages of logging issues in real time are clear and tangible:

Shorter response times

Logging an issue the moment it happens means it reaches the right person instantly. The time that used to get lost in handoffs, paperwork, or confirmations disappears, and problems get solved much faster.

More reliable data

Logging on the spot means the details are fresh: there's no risk of forgetting information, writing incomplete notes, or lacking proof. Plus, with photos or videos included, the evidence is objective and verifiable.

Better communication between operators and managers

Operators can communicate directly with supervisors or quality managers through the digital record. The flow is instant and doesn't depend on intermediaries.

Stronger traceability for audits

Real-time issues are logged with date, time, location, and evidence. This creates a transparent, complete history that makes any ISO, IFS, or BRC audit easier.

Together, these advantages make immediate logging a practice that not only speeds up day-to-day operations but also strengthens the organization's overall quality.

Use cases in factories

Managing issues in real time applies across multiple areas of an industrial plant. A few illustrative examples:

  • Production stopped due to a breakdown: when a machine stops, logging the issue with a photo or video lets the maintenance team act instantly.
  • Quality problem on the packaging line: if a packaging defect is detected, documenting it in real time ensures quick action is taken before the whole production run is affected.
  • Safety issues on the plant floor: an operator who spots a hazard can log the issue instantly, triggering alerts to prevent accidents.

These cases show that real-time issue management isn't limited to one area — it impacts production, quality, maintenance, and safety across the board.

Technical requirements

To successfully roll out the practice of logging issues in real time, factories need to consider several technical requirements:

  • Mobile apps compatible with different devices and operating systems.
  • Offline versions that let you keep logging even without a stable connection, with automatic syncing once coverage returns.
  • Data security and permissions: it's essential that records are protected and only authorized people can access the information.
  • Simple, fast interface so operators see logging not as a chore, but as a natural part of their daily work.

Meeting these requirements ensures the benefits of real-time issues are realized across the whole plant.

Common challenges and solutions

Implementing a real-time issue system also comes with challenges worth planning for:

  • Operator resistance: some workers may be reluctant to use mobile devices. The solution is simple training, intuitive interfaces, and showing how digital logging saves them work.
  • Limited connectivity: in areas of the plant with poor coverage, the system needs to work offline and sync afterward.
  • Standardizing records: if every operator logs issues differently, the data loses value. Defining categories, required fields, and templates ensures consistency.

When these challenges are handled properly, factories can move confidently toward an immediate, digital logging model.
