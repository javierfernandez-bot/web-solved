The management of real-time incidents is now a key necessity in modern factories. 

For years, incident records have been kept on paper or in hard-to-access systems, causing delays in communication, incomplete data and poor traceability. 

When an operator detected a problem, they often had to wait until the end of the shift to report it or rely on a supervisor to pass it on to the system. The result was clear: incidents reported late, accumulated errors and decisions that came too late.

The ability to log real-time incidents directly from a mobile phone or tablet has changed this dynamic. Now, factories can react quickly, reduce errors, improve traceability and make audits easier. In this article we'll look at what it means to log incidents in real time, what its advantages are, specific use cases, technical requirements, common challenges and how Solved makes this transformation possible.

What it means to log incidents in real time

Logging real-time incidents means that the details of a problem are entered at the very moment and place it happens. It's no longer about jotting it down on paper and passing it to a system later, but documenting immediately, with context and digital evidence, everything the owner needs to act without delay.

For this to be possible, three basic elements are needed:

  • Mobile devices or tablets that operators can use on the plant floor.
  • Connectivity suitable for data to sync instantly, though an offline option must exist.
  • A specialized application that allows logging, attaching photos and videos, and sending automatic alerts.

When these elements come together, the management of real-time incidents becomes a strategic tool for improving factory efficiency.

Advantages of immediate logging

The advantages of logging real-time incidents are clear and tangible:

Reduced response times

By logging an incident the moment it happens, it reaches the right owner immediately. The time previously lost on transfers, paperwork or confirmations disappears, and problems get resolved much faster.

More reliable data

Logging instantly means the details are fresh: it avoids the risk of forgetting information, writing it down incompletely, or lacking proof. Also, by including photos or videos, the evidence is objective and verifiable.

Better communication between operators and owners

Operators can communicate directly with supervisors or quality managers through the digital record. The flow is immediate and doesn't depend on intermediaries.

Greater traceability for audits

The real-time incidents are logged with date, time, location and evidence. This creates a transparent, complete history that makes any ISO audit easier, IFS o BRC.

Together, these advantages make immediate logging a practice that not only speeds up daily operations, but also strengthens the organization's overall quality.

Technical requirements

To successfully implement the practice of logging real-time incidents, factories need to consider several technical requirements:

  • Compatible mobile apps with different devices and operating systems.
  • Offline versions that allow logging to continue even without a stable connection, with automatic sync once coverage is restored.
  • Data security and permissions: it's essential that records are protected and only authorized people can access the information.
  • Simple, fast interface so operators don't see logging as a burden, but as a natural part of their daily work.

Meeting these requirements ensures that the benefits of real-time incidents are realized across the whole plant.

Common challenges and solutions

Implementing a system of real-time incidents also raises challenges worth planning for:

  • Operator resistance: some workers may be reluctant to use mobile devices. The solution lies in simple training, intuitive interfaces and showing how digital logging saves them work.
  • Limited connectivity: in plant areas with poor coverage, the system needs to work offline and sync afterwards.
  • Standardization of records: if each operator logs things differently, the data loses value. Defining categories, mandatory fields and templates ensures consistency.

When these challenges are addressed properly, factories can move confidently toward a model of immediate, digital logging.
