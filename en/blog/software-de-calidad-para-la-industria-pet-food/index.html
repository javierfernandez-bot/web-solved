Quality Software for the Pet Food Industry

The pet food market has grown steadily in recent years, and with it, regulatory pressure and consumer expectations have soared. Today, any plant producing wet food, kibble, treats or BARF diets needs quality software for pet food that lives up to the toughest standards. This isn't optional — it's an operational necessity that directly affects product safety, brand reputation and legal compliance.

Why the pet food industry demands the same rigor as human food, and sometimes more

There's a common assumption that pet food is subject to less strict regulation than products for human consumption. The reality is quite different. European legislation governing food hygiene and traceability applies across the board, and as set out in Regulation (EC) 178/2002 on general food law (Regulation [EC] 178/2002, 2002), feed business operators have the same traceability and safety obligations as operators in the human food sector. This includes the ability to identify and recall any batch that poses a risk.

What's more, today's consumers treat their pets as family members. Any quality crisis — a foreign object, microbiological contamination, an allergic reaction triggered by an undeclared ingredient — can spark a reputational storm as severe as one affecting an infant food brand. That's why internal rigor at pet food plants has grown exponentially: BRCGS certifications (BRCGS, n.d.), customer audits, retail technical specifications… the bar keeps rising.

On top of that comes the complexity of the raw materials. Pet food routinely uses meat by-products, animal-derived meals, fish oils and other ingredients with high microbiological risk that require specific controls at every point in the chain.

Critical points: meat-based raw materials, formulation and cross-allergen control

Any quality manager working at a pet food plant knows the critical points cluster around three main areas:

Animal-origin raw materials. Meat meals, poultry by-products or fish broths can arrive at the plant with significant variability between suppliers and batches. A quality control system for pet food must allow every incoming batch's test results to be logged and validated, automatically blocking any that fail to meet the specifications agreed with the supplier. Without digitization, this process turns into a sea of spreadsheets where a non-conforming batch can easily slip into production.

Formulation and product stability. Pet food formulas can change fairly often, especially when nutritional profiles are adjusted or ingredients are swapped for cost or availability reasons. Every formula change is a potential risk if it isn't managed with full traceability. The quality software must record which formula version was used for each batch produced and exactly which raw materials went into that batch.

Cross-allergen control. This is perhaps the most delicate point. Many pet food plants produce formulas with different animal proteins (chicken, lamb, salmon, beef) on the same lines. For animals with food sensitivities — and their owners, who specifically look for single-protein or grain-free products — cross-contamination is a real problem. Properly managing the cleaning and verification plan between reference changes, and documenting it in a traceable way, is essential. What quality software for pet food must cover: HACCP, batch traceability and issues on the plant floor

When we look at what a quality software for pet food plants really needs, the answer goes far beyond digitizing paper records. A truly useful system must bring the following modules together coherently:

Digital HACCP management. The Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point plan is the core of any food safety system. The software must allow identified hazards, CCPs and critical limits to be defined and linked to the daily control records operators complete on the floor. If a value falls out of range, the system must escalate the alert automatically, without depending on someone reviewing a paper sheet at the end of the shift.

Complete batch traceability. Pet food traceability must work in both directions: backward (which raw materials and suppliers are part of a finished-product batch) and forward (which customers and orders received that batch). This capability is critical for managing a product recall quickly and precisely, minimizing the scope and cost of the crisis.

Issue and non-conformity logging. Every deviation detected on the floor — whether an out-of-spec test result, a customer complaint or a faulty measuring instrument — must be logged, assigned to an owner and resolved within a set deadline. The software must make it easy to open corrective actions and track their effectiveness, generating evidence that can be presented in an audit.

Supplier control and approval. Given the inherent variability of animal-origin raw materials, the supplier module is especially relevant in pet food. The system must allow agreed specifications, incoming certificates of analysis and each supplier's history of non-compliance to be managed, supporting objective decisions on approval or disqualification.
