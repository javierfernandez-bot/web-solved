Issue Management: Excel vs. Specialized Software

By Claudia · December 3, 2025 · 8 min read

At many factories, software-based issue management still hasn't fully replaced the traditional spreadsheet-based method. Excel remains the most common tool thanks to its low cost and accessibility. But as industrial operations grow, regulatory requirements increase, and teams expand, managing issues in Excel stops being enough.

Manual logging, duplicate data, and a lack of traceability cause factories to lose time, productivity, and control. A specialized issue management software, on the other hand, automates processes, delivers real-time information, and ensures regulatory compliance with minimal effort.

In this article, we'll look at how issues are managed in Excel, its main limitations, the advantages of professional software, a direct comparison between both approaches, and how Solved overcomes every barrier of the traditional method.

How Issues Are Managed in Excel

For years, Excel has been the starting point for many companies looking to organize their information. Software-based issue management can seem like an unnecessary investment when you already have a familiar, free tool like Excel. But its simplicity is also its biggest weakness.

1. Familiarity and Low Cost

Excel is widely known across every administrative team. It requires no advanced training and, in most cases, no additional licenses. For a small factory, it's a fast way to start logging issues and doing basic tracking.

2. Total Flexibility

Every company can adapt its spreadsheets to its own processes: custom columns, colors, filters, and formulas. That initial freedom creates a sense of control over the data.

3. Easy File Sharing

In small settings, sharing a file over email or an internal network can seem good enough. But as the number of issues grows, this practice becomes a source of errors and wasted time.

In short, Excel works fine in the early stages, but it lacks the structure needed to handle the complexity of a modern industrial plant.

Excel's Limitations in Issue Management

Although Excel seems practical, it has critical limitations that directly affect traceability, data quality, and regulatory compliance. Specialized issue management software solves exactly these problems.

1. Human Error and Duplicates

Issues have to be entered manually, which increases the chance of typos, wrong dates, or duplicate records. On top of that, when different departments work from different versions of the same file, the data stops being consistent.

2. Lack of Traceability

A spreadsheet can't tell you who changed a record or when. That's a serious problem during audits, which require evidence that every issue was tracked. Without traceability, you can't prove your quality system actually works.

3. Difficulty During Audits

Searching for old issues, corrective actions, or evidence can take hours or even days. Software-based issue management removes this problem by centralizing all the information and generating instant reports.

4. No Real-Time Work on the Plant Floor

Excel is designed for office computers, not the industrial environment. Operators can't log issues from their phone or tablet, so data gets captured after the problem has already occurred. This causes delays and lost context.

5. Lack of Visibility and Analysis

Excel can't build automatic dashboards or smart alerts. Information stays static and doesn't help you spot patterns or trends. Software-based issue management, by contrast, shows up-to-date metrics that support data-driven decisions.

Advantages of Specialized Software

Implementing specialized issue management software completely transforms how a factory controls, analyzes, and resolves problems. Unlike Excel, these platforms are built to deliver full traceability, speed, and security at every step.

1. Logging From a Phone or Tablet

Operators can log issues at the exact moment they occur, with photos or videos as evidence. This enables real-time management, avoiding duplication and delays.

2. Digital evidence and automatic traceability
 Every action is logged with a date, time, and user. This ensures a complete history for each issue — ideal for audits and certifications (ISO, IFS, BRC, etc.).

3. Integration With ERP, MES, and CMMS

Specialized software connects issue management to your existing business systems, ensuring data consistency. This avoids logging the same information in multiple places — a common problem with Excel.

4. Automatic Reports and Visual Dashboards

Software-based issue management lets you generate reports and charts with key indicators (KPIs) in seconds: average resolution time, open issues, recurring causes, or deadline compliance.

5. Fewer Errors and Higher Productivity

Automating assignments, reminders, and workflows eliminates human error and improves response speed. Owners receive automatic notifications for every status change.

6. Simplified Regulatory Compliance

Thanks to digital traceability and complete documentation, audits move faster and reports are generated automatically, with no extra effort.

The Hidden Costs of Using Excel

One of the most common mistakes is assuming Excel is "free." Even though it requires no upfront investment, the lack of automation generates hidden costs that hurt profitability and productivity.

1. Time Lost Consolidating Information

Teams spend hours gathering data from different files, checking for duplicates, and building manual reports.

2. Data Entry Errors

One wrong number or a shifted cell can invalidate an entire report. Fixing these errors means rework and a loss of confidence in the data.

3. Poor Visibility

Without dashboards or alerts, managers don't know the real status of open issues. This delays decision-making and drags out resolution times.

4. Impact on Audits

Searching for evidence or proving traceability with Excel files can take days and pile on the pressure for the team. With issue management software, that information is always available with a single click.
