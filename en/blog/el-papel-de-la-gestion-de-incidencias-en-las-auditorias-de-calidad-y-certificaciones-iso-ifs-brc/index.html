Issue Management in ISO, IFS and BRC Audits

By Claudia · December 8, 2025 · 8 min read

In today's industrial environment, where excellence and regulatory compliance are essential to stay competitive, quality audits and international certifications such as ISO, IFS or BRC are a core part of operations. These standards don't just guarantee product quality — they also ensure consistency, traceability and continuous improvement within the organization.

Yet many factories still run into the same obstacle during audits: weak, scattered or manual issue management. Auditors ask for concrete evidence, and teams end up losing hours hunting for records, filling out paperwork, or trying to justify corrective actions that were never documented.

In this article we'll look at how issue management becomes a strategic tool for passing audits and certifications such as ISO 9001, IFS Food or BRCGS, the benefits of having a robust digital system in place, the most common mistakes made during audits, and how a platform like Solved simplifies the whole process.

How issues connect to quality audits

Every quality audit — whether internal or external — shares a common goal: to verify that processes are under control, that non-conformities are managed properly, and that there is full traceability of issues.

Auditors don't just focus on final outcomes — they focus on the process of detecting, logging, analyzing and resolving issues. Strong issue management shows that the organization reacts to problems, analyzes them and prevents recurrence, meeting the continuous-improvement principle required by ISO, IFS and BRC standards.

For example, during an audit, an auditor may ask for:

  • Evidence of the record for a specific issue and its corrective actions.
  • Detection, follow-up and closure dates.
  • Identification of the person responsible.
  • Confirmation that the action taken prevented recurrence.

If this data is presented clearly and digitally, the audit flows smoothly. But if records are scattered, incomplete or on paper, the process slows down and generates findings.

Benefits of robust issue management for audits

Having a solid issue management system for quality audits and ISO, IFS or BRC certifications brings significant advantages, both in operational efficiency and documentary compliance.

1. Full traceability of every issue
 Traceability is one of the fundamental pillars of any quality standard. With digital issue management, every event is logged with a date, time, owner and associated evidence. This lets you show the auditor that processes are controlled and verifiable.

2. A complete history with evidence
 A digital system lets you attach photos, videos, lab reports or meeting minutes directly to each issue. That way, all the information is centralized and accessible in one place — eliminating the need to dig through physical folders or old emails during the audit.

3. Less time spent on documentation prep
 Companies that manage issues manually can spend days preparing for an audit. With specialized software, reports are generated automatically and presented in seconds. The audit stops being a stressful process and becomes a simple review of evidence.

4. Continuous improvement and non-conformity prevention
 Robust issue management doesn't just help you pass audits — it also improves internal quality. By analyzing root causes and applying effective corrective actions, the organization reduces errors and demonstrates a real commitment to continuous improvement.

5. Simplified compliance across multiple standards
 A digital system lets you structure issue records according to the requirements of different standards (ISO 9001, ISO 22000, IFS, BRCGS), avoiding duplication and ensuring consistency in the information presented to auditors or customers.

How issue management connects to ISO, IFS and BRC standards

Each standard has its own requirements, but they all agree on one point: proper management of issues and corrective actions is essential to obtain and maintain certification.

Let's look at how this applies to the main certifications:

ISO 9001 – Quality Management System

ISO 9001 requires organizations to identify, control and analyze non-conformities, as well as demonstrate the effectiveness of the actions taken to prevent recurrence.

Digital issue management makes it possible to:

  • Log every non-conformity in detail.
  • Assign owners and follow-up dates.
  • Attach evidence and verifications.
  • Generate automatic reports for internal and external audits.

With this traceability, the auditor can review the full lifecycle of an issue: detection, root cause analysis, corrective action and verification of the outcome.

IFS Food – Food safety and product quality

IFS Food requires a documented system that guarantees full traceability of issues and the fast implementation of corrective actions.

Strong issue management in this context enables you to:

  • Document failures in raw materials, production or storage.
  • Link automatic alerts to critical issues.
  • Verify that correction deadlines are being met.
  • Make sure issues are resolved before they affect the final product.

IFS auditors also look favorably on companies with centralized digital systems, since this reflects control, efficiency and a genuine commitment to food safety.

BRCGS – Global food safety standards

The BRCGS standard places particular emphasis on preventing non-conformities and documenting every corrective and preventive action (CAPA).

Digital issue management makes it easier to:

  • Track each issue from detection through to closure.
  • Validate that the action taken was effective.
  • Analyze trends in recurring issues.
  • Generate automatic reports for management review.

In a BRCGS audit, the ability to show this information immediately and verifiably can be the difference between a minor conformity and a critical non-conformity.

Common challenges in quality audits

Even though international standards set out clear guidelines, many factories keep making the same mistakes during audits. Most stem from manual, decentralized or inefficient issue management.

1. Incomplete or outdated manual documentation
 Using spreadsheets, paper forms or scattered emails leads to lost information. During the audit, auditors find incomplete or inconsistent records.

2. Errors in the records
 Wrong dates, misassigned owners or vague descriptions raise doubts about how reliable the system really is. This often leads to findings or the need for follow-up audits.

3. Lack of follow-through on corrective actions
 Many issues get logged but never properly closed. Auditors spot open actions or ones with no verification evidence, which hurts the final rating.

4. Lack of cross-departmental traceability
 When each department (quality, production, maintenance) manages its own issues separately, there's no overall view of the system. This makes it harder to demonstrate control and consistency during the audit.
