What Are HACCP Prerequisite Programs (PRPs)

By Claudia · July 19, 2026 · 6 min read

What Are HACCP Prerequisite Programs (PRPs)

When we talk about food safety in the food industry, we tend to focus on the HACCP system. But before that system can work effectively, there's a fundamental layer that supports it: prerequisite programs (PRPs). Without well-established prerequisite programs, any HACCP plan is exposed to structural failures that no critical control point can compensate for. In this article we explain what PRPs are, how they're classified, and how to manage them efficiently.

What PRPs are and why they support the HACCP system

Prerequisite programs (PRPs) are the set of basic conditions and activities a food business must have under control before designing and implementing its Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) system. Their purpose is to create a hygienic, safe working environment that minimizes the likelihood of food hazards appearing.

A useful analogy: think of HACCP as a building. PRPs are the foundation. No matter how sophisticated the structure above, if the foundation is weak, the whole building shakes. Prerequisite plans aren't a bureaucratic formality — they're the guarantee that production processes start from proper hygienic conditions.

European law requires food business operators to implement and maintain these programs. As established by Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 on the hygiene of foodstuffs (Regulation [EC] 852/2004, 2004), every business must apply procedures based on HACCP principles, which necessarily means having the prior conditions — the PRPs — in place to guarantee hygiene at every stage of the food chain.

In practical terms, prerequisite programs range from facility cleaning to staff training, water control, and equipment maintenance. They cut across the entire production process, and managing them well significantly reduces the burden of hazards that HACCP will need to analyze.

The most common types of PRPs in the food industry

PRPs vary by company type and sector, but there's a set of prerequisite programs common to nearly any food facility. Here are the most relevant:

Cleaning and disinfection: One of the most critical prerequisite plans. It covers procedures for cleaning and disinfecting facilities, equipment, utensils and food-contact surfaces. It must define the products used, concentrations, frequencies and responsible staff.

Pest control: The presence of insects, rodents or other vectors can compromise product safety. A good pest control program sets out preventive measures (sealing access points, trapping systems) and reactive measures (actions upon detection), always documented and verified.

Staff training and hygiene: People are a contamination vector if they aren't properly trained. This PRP governs hygiene training, good handling practices, correct PPE use, and access rules for production areas.

Facility and equipment maintenance: Equipment in poor condition can create physical, chemical or microbiological hazards. The preventive and corrective maintenance plan ensures surfaces, machinery and structures don't pose a risk to the product.

Water control: Water is an ingredient or a processing agent in most food industries. This PRP ensures the water used is potable and that periodic analytical checks confirm its microbiological and chemical quality.

Allergen management and supplier control: These carry increasing weight among prerequisite plans. The first prevents cross-contact with allergens; the second ensures raw materials arrive in proper safety and hygiene conditions.

Traceability and waste management: The ability to trace a product through the food chain is both a legal and operational requirement, complemented by proper waste disposal that prevents contamination.

Difference between PRPs and oPRPs (operational prerequisite programs)

A common point of confusion for quality managers is distinguishing standard PRPs from so-called operational prerequisite programs (oPRPs). The difference is subtle but important, especially when working under standards like ISO 22000 (ISO, n.d.).

General PRPs control production environment conditions broadly. Failing to meet them creates a risk situation, but isn't directly tied to a specific hazard identified in the hazard analysis. For example, a general floor cleaning plan or a basic training program.

oPRPs (operational prerequisite programs), on the other hand, are control measures identified during hazard analysis that don't manage a critical control point (CCP) but do control significant hazards at a specific process step. The key difference from a CCP is that an oPRP doesn't have a critical limit measured with the same precision, although it does have defined action criteria.

In practice, oPRPs act as an intermediate category between general PRPs and HACCP's CCPs. They're more specific measures tied to concrete steps in the production process. Identifying and documenting them correctly is essential so the HACCP system stays robust without becoming overloaded with an excess of hard-to-manage critical points.
