The incident management in industry can only improve if it is measured accurately. 

In production environments where every minute counts, the Incident management KPIs are the essential tool for evaluating performance, operational efficiency and team responsiveness. Without objective data, it's impossible to know whether processes really work or whether the corrective actions are delivering results.

Measuring through key indicators not only helps detect failures, but also anticipate them. The Incident management KPIs in industry offer a clear view of the health of the quality and maintenance system, helping reduce downtime, optimize resources and improve traceability.

In this article you'll learn what KPIs are, which ones are most relevant for measuring effectiveness in incident management, how to interpret them and how tools like Solved make it easier to visualize them in real time.

What are incident management KPIs

The Incident management KPIs in industry (Key Performance Indicators) are metrics that make it possible to quantify the performance of a process or system. Their main function is to measure efficiency, quality and compliance, three fundamental pillars for ensuring controlled industrial operations.

These indicators help answer key questions:

  • How fast do we resolve an incident?
  • How often do the same problems recur?
  • What percentage of incidents are critical and require immediate attention?
  • How much do incidents really cost the organization?

In short, the Incident management KPIs provide objective evidence for making decisions, prioritizing actions and promoting a culture of continuous improvement based on data.

Most relevant KPIs for measuring effectiveness

The most important indicators are not the most numerous, but the most representative. Below are the Incident management KPIs in industry most used by quality, maintenance and operations departments.

1. Mean Time to Resolution (MTTR)

This is one of the Incident management KPIs most critical. It measures the average time elapsed from when an incident is detected until its closure.

  • Formula: MTTR = Σ (Resolution time) / Total closed incidents
  • Interpretation: a high MTTR indicates slowness or lack of resources; a low one reflects efficiency and good coordination.
    Reducing this value involves optimizing workflows and automating assignments.

2. Number of recurring incidents

This KPI measures how many incidents recur over a given period. It's essential for evaluating the effectiveness of corrective and preventive actions.

  • Goal: reducing recurrences shows that problems are being resolved at their root cause.
  • Use: it helps identify areas, machines or shifts with the highest repeat failure rate.

3. Percentage of critical incidents

Not all incidents have the same impact. This KPI shows the proportion of incidents classified as critical out of the total.

  • Formula: (Critical incidents / Total incidents) × 100
  • Interpretation: a high percentage can indicate a lack of preventive maintenance or deficiencies in process control.

4. Average cost per incident

Every incident has an associated cost: labor, materials, downtime, rework or returns.

  • Formula: Total incident cost / Number of incidents
  • Value: it helps quantify the economic impact of management and justify investments in automation or training.

5. On-time closure rate (SLAs met)

This KPI measures the percentage of incidents resolved within the defined target time (SLA).

  • Formula: (Incidents closed on time / Total incidents) × 100
  • Target: keep the value above 90%. A decrease indicates bottlenecks or work overload.

6. Completed corrective and preventive actions (CAPA)

It makes it possible to assess the effectiveness of the measures implemented after incidents.

  • Formula: Closed CAPAs / Total CAPAs × 100
  • Interpretation: a high percentage reflects commitment to the continuous improvement and maturity in the quality system.

7. Incidents by area or shift

Breaking down incidents by production area or shift is key to detecting patterns.

  • Use: identifying when or where more incidents occur helps focus efforts and allocate resources correctly.

Each of these Incident management KPIs can be adapted to the type of industry, but they all share the same goal: providing a quantitative view of operational performance.

How to interpret incident management KPIs

Measuring without interpreting correctly can lead to wrong conclusions. The Incident management KPIs in industry must be analyzed in context, comparing periods, areas and predefined targets.

High or low values: what they really mean

  • A High MTTR can be due to a lack of automation, excessive workload or errors in assigning owners.
  • A high percentage of critical incidents can indicate deficiencies in preventive maintenance.
  • Low costs per incident are not always positive if they imply under-reporting or underestimation of resources.

Trend detection

The Incident management KPIs make sense when observed over time. If a trend shows a steady reduction in MTTR and in recurrences, it means the quality system is maturing. Conversely, if costs or closure times increase, processes or responsibilities need to be reviewed.

Internal comparisons

Analyzing KPIs across areas, shifts or production lines helps uncover imbalances. For example, a line with an MTTR 40% higher than another could point to communication failures or a lack of training.

How to use KPIs to improve processes

The Incident management KPIs in industry are not just numbers: they are the basis for continuous improvement.

1. Identifying bottlenecks

Analyzing resolution times or closure rates helps find points where incidents pile up unattended. With this information, workflows can be redesigned and resources reallocated.

2. Setting measurable targets

Each KPI should have an associated target: reducing MTTR by 20%, lowering recurrence by 15% or raising SLA compliance to 95%. The Incident management KPIs transform abstract goals into concrete, achievable objectives.

3. Comparing areas or shifts

The visibility that indicators provide makes internal comparison easier. This fosters healthy competition between teams and allows best practices to be shared.

4. Data-based prevention

When the Incident management KPIs show repeating patterns, it becomes possible to anticipate and prevent future failures. This drives predictive maintenance and reduces costs in the long run.

5. Effective communication with management

A dashboard with clear KPIs makes it possible to present measurable results to management. Instead of lengthy reports, leaders can see the effectiveness of incident management in seconds and make strategic decisions.
