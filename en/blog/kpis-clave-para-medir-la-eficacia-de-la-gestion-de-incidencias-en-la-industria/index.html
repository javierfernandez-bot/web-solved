Key KPIs for issue management

By Claudia · December 1, 2025 · 7 min read

Issue management in industry can only improve if it's measured precisely. 

In production environments where every minute counts, issue management KPIs are the essential tool for evaluating performance, operational efficiency and team responsiveness. Without objective data, it's impossible to know whether processes are really working or whether corrective actions are delivering results.

Measuring through key indicators doesn't just help detect failures — it helps get ahead of them. Issue management KPIs in industry give a clear picture of the health of the quality and maintenance system, helping reduce downtime, optimize resources and improve traceability.

In this article you'll learn what KPIs are, which are the most relevant for measuring the effectiveness of issue management, how to interpret them, and how tools like Solved make it easy to see them in real time.

What issue management KPIs are

Issue management KPIs in industry (Key Performance Indicators) are metrics that let you quantify the performance of a process or system. Their main job is to measure efficiency, quality and compliance — three pillars essential to keeping industrial operations under control.

These indicators help answer key questions:

  • How fast do we resolve an issue?
  • How often do the same problems recur?
  • What percentage of issues are critical and need immediate attention?
  • What do issues actually cost the organization?

In short, issue management KPIs provide objective evidence for making decisions, prioritizing actions and promoting a data-driven continuous improvement culture.

The most relevant KPIs for measuring effectiveness

The most important indicators aren't the most numerous ones — they're the most representative. Below are the issue management KPIs in industry most widely used by quality, maintenance and operations departments.

1. Mean Time to Resolution (MTTR)

One of the most critical issue management KPIs. It measures the average time from when an issue is detected to when it's closed.

  • Formula: MTTR = Σ (resolution time) / total closed issues
  • Interpretation: a high MTTR points to slowness or a lack of resources; a low one reflects efficiency and good coordination.
    Reducing this value means streamlining workflows and automating assignments.

2. Number of recurring issues

This KPI measures how many issues repeat over a given period. It's essential for evaluating how effective corrective and preventive actions really are.

  • Goal: reducing recurrence shows problems are being resolved at their root cause.
  • Use: lets you identify areas, machines or shifts with more repeat failures.

3. Percentage of critical issues

Not every issue has the same impact. This KPI shows the proportion of issues classified as critical out of the total.

  • Formula: (critical issues / total issues) × 100
  • Interpretation: a high percentage can point to a lack of preventive maintenance or gaps in process control.

4. Average cost per issue

Every issue has an associated cost: labor, materials, downtime, rework or returns.

  • Formula: total issue cost / number of issues
  • Value: helps quantify the economic impact of issue management and justify investment in automation or training.

5. On-time closure rate (SLA compliance)

This KPI measures the percentage of issues resolved within the defined target time (SLA).

  • Formula: (issues closed on time / total issues) × 100
  • Target: keep the value above 90%. A drop signals bottlenecks or team overload.

6. Corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) completed

Lets you evaluate how effective the measures implemented after issues actually are.

  • Formula: closed CAPAs / total CAPAs × 100
  • Interpretation: a high percentage reflects commitment to continuous improvement and a mature quality system.

7. Issues by area or shift

Breaking down issues by production area or shift is key to spotting patterns.

  • Use: identifying when or where more issues occur helps focus effort and allocate resources correctly.

Each of these issue management KPIs can be adapted to different types of industry, but they all share the same goal: giving a quantitative view of operational performance.

How to interpret issue management KPIs

Measuring without interpreting correctly can lead to the wrong conclusions. Issue management KPIs in industry need to be analyzed in context, comparing periods, areas and predefined targets.

High or low values: what they really mean

  • A high MTTR can stem from a lack of automation, excess workload, or errors in assigning owners.
  • A high percentage of critical issues can point to gaps in preventive maintenance.
  • Low cost per issue isn't always a good sign — it can mean incomplete records or underestimated resources.

Spotting trends

Issue management KPIs only make sense when tracked over time. If a trend shows a steady drop in MTTR and recurrence, it means the quality system is maturing. Conversely, if costs or closure times rise, it's time to review processes or ownership.

Internal comparisons

Comparing KPIs across areas, shifts or production lines reveals imbalances. For example, a line with an MTTR 40% higher than another could point to communication gaps or a lack of training.

How to use KPIs to improve processes

Issue management KPIs in industry aren't just numbers — they're the foundation for continuous improvement.

1. Identifying bottlenecks

Analyzing resolution times or closure rates helps find where issues are piling up unattended. With this information, workflows can be redesigned and resources reassigned.

2. Setting measurable goals

Every KPI should have a goal attached: cut MTTR by 20%, lower recurrence by 15%, or raise SLA compliance to 95%. Issue management KPIs turn abstract targets into concrete, achievable goals.

3. Comparing across areas or shifts

The visibility indicators provide makes internal comparison easier, encouraging healthy competition between teams and the sharing of best practices.

4. Data-driven prevention

When issue management KPIs show recurring patterns, it becomes possible to anticipate and prevent future failures. This drives predictive maintenance and cuts costs in the long run.

5. Effective communication with management

A dashboard with clear KPIs makes it possible to present measurable results to leadership. Instead of lengthy reports, leaders can see the effectiveness of issue management in seconds and make strategic decisions.
