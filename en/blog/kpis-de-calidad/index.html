What KPIs should a quality director watch

By Karim · November 11, 2025 · 6 min read

One of the most relevant questions in modern factory management is what essential data every quality director should watch to make fast, evidence-based decisions aligned with international standards. In an industrial environment where issues come up daily, customers demand full traceability, and audits (ISO, IFS, BRC) are getting stricter by the year, data becomes the engine of efficiency and continuous improvement.

The quality director's role is no longer limited to overseeing controls or reviewing monthly reports. Their job is to lead decision-making with real-time information, turning quality management into a competitive advantage. To do that, they need to know which indicators to prioritize, how to interpret them, and which tools to use to visualize the information clearly and accessibly.

Why data is key for a quality director

Industrial digitization has multiplied the sources of information. But more data doesn't always mean better management. What makes the difference is identifying what essential data every quality director should watch and using it strategically.

The benefits of having visibility into the right data are clear:

  • Better preparedness for external audits (ISO 9001, ISO 22000, IFS, BRCGS).
  • The ability to get ahead of critical issues before they affect production.
  • Better communication with senior management and customers thanks to objective information.
  • Optimized resources and lower costs.

Essential data for a quality director

The list of data flowing through a factory is endless, but there's a core set of indicators that's essential for any quality manager.

Open and closed issues

A quality director needs to know in real time how many issues are active, what state they're in, and how long they've been open. This data shows the organization's responsiveness and how effective its resolution processes are.

Resolution times

Knowing how many issues exist isn't enough — it's crucial to measure how long the factory takes to resolve them. This indicator is one of the most relevant when it comes to what essential data every quality director should watch, because it directly affects productivity and customer satisfaction.

Corrective and preventive actions

Tracking corrective actions and preventive ones (CAPA) shows whether the organization is learning from mistakes or simply reacting to problems. Knowing what percentage of actions are completed on time and which ones are delayed is vital for continuous improvement.

Internal audit results

Every internal audit finding is a signal worth analyzing. The quality director needs a clear summary of major and minor non-conformities and improvement opportunities. This data helps anticipate problems before external audits.

Critical process indicators

Every sector has processes that determine the final product's quality. For the food industry, it might be temperature control during pasteurization; for a pharmaceutical company, batch sterility; for automotive, component calibration. Identifying and monitoring these indicators is essential.

Customer complaints and returns

External data matters just as much as internal data. A quality director needs to analyze how many complaints come in, what their causes are, and what impact they have on brand perception.

Suppliers and raw materials

Answering what essential data every quality director should watch also means reviewing supplier reliability. Knowing how many issues stem from defective raw materials, or what percentage of suppliers pass audits, is essential for securing the supply chain.

How to visualize the data: the role of dashboards

Many quality directors aren't technology experts, but they need clear, real-time information. That's where dashboards come in.

A quality dashboard is simply a tool that brings together the essential data every quality director should watch in a visual, easy-to-read format. The most important indicators it should include are:

  • Number of open and closed issues.
  • Average resolution time.
  • Corrective actions in progress.
  • Issue trends by line, shift or type.
  • Internal audit results.
  • Critical process KPIs.

This way, the quality director doesn't need to go through hundreds of reports or spreadsheets — they can spot priorities at a glance.

How to use data for continuous improvement

Having access to data isn't enough — what matters is how it's used. The continuous improvement cycle (Plan, Do, Check, Act – PDCA) depends on having reliable information at every stage.

  • Plan: define quality objectives based on historical data.
  • Do: implement process changes.
  • Check: measure results against the defined indicators.
  • Act: adjust strategies based on the results obtained.

Answering what essential data every quality director should watch makes clear that information is the starting point for this cycle, which in turn secures regulatory compliance and competitive advantage.

Common challenges in using data

Although the theory is clear, in practice many directors run into problems:

  • Data scattered across different systems (ERP, MES, CMMS).
  • Incomplete information due to paper records.
  • Delays in consolidating reports.
  • A lack of analytical culture in some teams.

These challenges explain why so many factories spend weeks preparing for audits instead of being ready within hours.

Conclusion

Knowing what essential data every quality director should watch is fundamental to leading effectively in modern industrial environments.
