How Much Does Issue Management Software Cost

If you're considering digitizing non-conformity control at your plant, the first thing you want to know is the issue management software price. And that makes complete sense: before you bring management on board or ask for a formal quote, you need a ballpark figure to know whether the investment fits your budget. In this article, we explain what factors determine the cost, what licensing models exist, why Excel and WhatsApp have a hidden price much higher than it looks, and how to calculate whether the tool pays for itself.

What factors determine the price of issue management software

There's no single flat rate. The cost of an issue management tool depends on several variables worth understanding before you compare quotes:

Included features. A basic solution that only logs issues costs less than a platform that also includes root cause analysis, action plans, deadline tracking, dashboards, and document traceability. For a quality manager in the food industry, advanced features aren't a luxury — they're what prevents deviations from recurring and demonstrates continuous improvement during an audit.

Number of users. Many vendors charge per active user per month. The more operators, technicians, and managers who need access to the system, the higher your monthly bill. In plant environments with multiple shifts and different areas, this model can make the solution much more expensive.

Deployment type. SaaS (cloud-based) solutions usually have a monthly or annual fee with no large upfront investment; on-premise solutions require your own infrastructure, perpetual licenses, and in-house maintenance, which drives up the total cost of ownership.

Support and training. Some vendors include onboarding, training, and support in the base price; others charge separately for it. In the food industry, where quality teams have little time for a learning curve, initial support is key and should be spelled out clearly in the quote.

Integrations. If you need to connect your issue management tool to your ERP, your document management system, or supplier tools, integrations can add extra development or connector costs.

Generally speaking, specialized food-industry quality solutions range from a few hundred euros a month for small and mid-size companies to several thousand for multi-plant corporate rollouts. The key is to evaluate the total cost, not just the base fee.

Issue management software price: per-user vs. unlimited users, and what works best for a factory

The most common pricing model in the SaaS market is pay per user. At first glance it seems fair: you only pay for who uses the tool. In a food factory, though, this model creates real practical problems.

Think about how an issue actually gets handled on the plant floor: a line operator spots it, the shift technician validates it, the quality manager analyzes it, the production manager closes it, and management reviews it in the monthly report. That's five people involved in a single record. If you pay per person, the monthly per-user price multiplies fast and can become hard to justify to a management team focused on cost control.

The unlimited users, flat rate per plant model is far more predictable and scalable for industrial settings. You know exactly what you'll pay each month, no matter how many people are involved in the quality workflow. This makes broad adoption much easier: if the price doesn't go up with each new user, there's no incentive to restrict access, and information flows better.

When evaluating quotes, always ask: what happens if I give access to every operator across all three shifts? Does the price multiply? That answer will tell you a lot about what the tool will actually cost you once it's fully rolled out.

The hidden cost of sticking with Excel, email, or WhatsApp

One of the most common arguments against investing in an issue management tool is: "we already handle it fine with Excel and email." It's understandable, but that "no cost" is actually one of the highest costs in the quality department — it just doesn't show up on any invoice.

How much time does your team spend each week digging through three-month-old emails to prove an issue was properly closed? How many hours go into preparing manual reports for internal or customer audits? How many times has a deviation recurred because the corrective action got buried in a WhatsApp thread nobody reviewed? That time has a real cost in salaries and, above all, in risk.

From a regulatory standpoint, traceability and non-conformity management are enforceable requirements. As set out in Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 on general food law (Regulation [EC] 178/2002, 2002), food business operators must be able to quickly identify and report any food safety problem. Demonstrating that compliance with records scattered across emails and spreadsheets isn't just inefficient — during an inspection or a food safety crisis, it can become a serious problem.

What's more, the industry's most demanding certification standards — such as BRCGS Global Standards (BRCGS, n.d.) — require documented, traceable evidence of issue and corrective action management. If your current system doesn't generate that evidence automatically, the hidden cost also includes the risk of audit findings.

Add up your team's time, the regulatory risk, recurring issues due to poor follow-up, and the reputational cost of a failure that could have been avoided. That's the real price of not having the right tool.

How to calculate the return and request a Solved demo

Before requesting a formal quote, we recommend doing a quick return-on-investment calculation. Answer these questions:

How many issues does your team handle per month? If it's 30 and each one takes an average of 45 minutes between logging, follow-up, and closing, you're spending more than 22 hours a month purely on administrative issue management. At a quality technician's hourly cost, that number adds up fast.

How many of those issues recur? Recurrence is the clearest sign that corrective actions aren't being followed through. A single recurring issue affecting finished product can cost far more in a recall, rework, or customer complaint than a year of subscription to any software.

How much time do you spend preparing reports? With a centralized system, reports are generated automatically. If you save four hours a month on report preparation, you've already accounted for part of the return.

With those numbers on the table, the conversation with management changes completely: you're no longer asking for an expense — you're presenting an investment with a measurable return.
