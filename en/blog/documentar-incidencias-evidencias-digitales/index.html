The need for documenting digital incidents in the plant is more pressing today than ever. Plants that still rely on paper records, printed spreadsheets, or handwritten notes face a constant risk: lost documents, incomplete records, information that takes too long to reach the people responsible, and delays in audits or certifications. In an industrial setting where every minute counts, this method is no longer enough.

Digital evidence —photos, videos, audio recordings, or attached documents— offers a practical, powerful solution. It makes it possible to record what happens in real time, brings traceability to processes, and ensures information flows instantly between teams. In this article we'll look at what digital evidence actually is, the benefits of adopting it, best practices for using it in the plant, how to integrate it with existing systems, and how tools like Solved simplify the whole process.

What digital evidence in incidents is

To understand why it is so important documenting digital incidentsit's worth defining what we mean by evidence in an industrial setting. These are objective proofs that accompany the record of an incident, non-conformity or relevant event at the plant. This evidence confirms what happened and makes both analysis and resolution easier.

Among the most common types are:

  • Photosimages showing the fault on a production line, a defect in a product, or an anomaly in a piece of equipment.
  • Videosrecordings that make it possible to observe processes in motion, repeated faults, or behavior that's hard to put into words.
  • Audio recordingsuseful for recording quick observations or operator statements without having to write anything down.
  • Attached technical documentsdrawings, maintenance reports, lab results, or product data sheets.

The key is that this digital evidence provides traceability. It's not just recorded that an incident occurred, but how it was spotted, who detected it, and what evidence is available to follow up on it. For ISO audits, IFS or BRC, this detail makes the difference between passing an inspection or not.

Benefits of Documenting Incidents Digitally

Adopting a system for documenting digital incidents brings clear advantages that outweigh any upfront investment.

  • Greater data accuracyevidence removes ambiguity. A photo of a defective batch or a video of a failing machine carries more weight than a brief written description.
  • Faster communicationincidents reach the responsible person instantly, with full detail. This cuts response times and avoids confusion.
  • Easier auditsauditors can review records backed by objective evidence. This saves hours spent hunting for paperwork or unclear explanations.
  • Evidence-based decisionsa quality manager can make decisions based on visual, verifiable information instead of relying on subjective interpretations.

What's more, centralizing digital evidence makes it possible to analyze it over time, spot patterns, and prevent recurrence.

Best Practices for Capturing Evidence in the Plant

Having the ability to documenting digital incidentsis not enough on its own; best practices need to be applied so the information recorded is actually useful.

  1. Use of mobile devices and tablets
     Allow operators and supervisors to log incidents directly on the plant floor using their phone or tablet. This ensures immediacy and removes the need to transfer information to another system afterward.
  2. Always attach context
     Each piece of evidence must be accompanied by basic information: date and time, location, production line, owner, and incident category. A photo without context loses value in an audit.
  3. Keep formats consistent
     Define standards for photo or video quality, file naming, and the type of documents that can be attached. This makes later retrieval easier.
  4. Secure, accessible storage
     Evidence should be stored in the cloud or on a central server with controlled access permissions. This prevents losses and guarantees availability even in external audits.

These practices ensure that documenting digital incidents is an efficient task that adds real value to the quality team.

Integrating digital evidence with existing systems

A key aspect in maximizing the value of documenting digital incidents is integration with the plant's other systems. Evidence shouldn't stay isolated in a single piece of software; it should flow to the systems that need it.

  • With ERP: incidents can be linked to batches, orders, or customers, creating a dynamic, consistent database.
  • With MES: faults detected in production are linked to work orders or specific lines.
  • With CMMS: evidence supports maintenance interventions and records repairs.

Common challenges and how to solve them

Although the benefits are clear, many plants face obstacles when implementing the practice of documenting digital incidents.

  • Connectivity issues: in some plants, WiFi or data coverage isn't stable. The solution is to use applications that work offline and sync afterward.
  • Large files: high-resolution videos or photos take up space. We recommend automatic compression or size limits for each file.
  • Team resistance: some operators may see digital logging as extra work. The key is simple training and showing the direct benefits.
  • Data privacy: photos or videos may show sensitive information. It's essential to set clear permissions and access policies.

Overcoming these challenges ensures a smooth transition to digitalization.
