Quality and Food Safety Glossary

Dictionary of key terms in traceability, quality and factory operations for the food industry.

A

Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)

Methodology to eliminate the root cause of a non-conformity (corrective) and prevent it from happening again (preventive). It structures continuous improvement and is a common requirement in certification audits.

View term →

A

Allergens

Substances that can trigger adverse reactions (gluten, tree nuts, dairy, etc.). Managing them requires cross-contamination control, accurate labeling and cleaning validation between production runs.

View term →

R

Root Cause Analysis

Technique to identify the real origin of a problem (5 Whys, Ishikawa) instead of just treating its symptoms. It prevents the same issue from recurring and reduces the cost of poor quality.

View term →

H

HACCP

Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points system. An internationally recognized preventive methodology to identify, assess and control food safety risks at every stage of the production process.

View term →

Q

Quality Audit

Systematic, independent assessment that verifies processes meet standards, internal procedures and legal requirements. It can be internal, customer-led or for certification (IFS, BRCGS, ISO).

View term →

I

Internal Audit

Assessment carried out by the organization itself to check its management system ahead of external audits. It catches deviations early and demonstrates the maturity of the quality system.

View term →

B

BRCGS

Brand Reputation Compliance Global Standards. An internationally recognized food safety certification scheme, especially demanded by the UK market and large retailers.

View term →

C

Cross-Contamination

Unintended transfer of contaminants or allergens between products, surfaces or equipment. Preventing it requires cleaning procedures, flow segregation and verification records.

View term →

P

Process Control

Monitoring and recording critical parameters (temperature, weight, pH, time) during manufacturing to ensure the product meets specifications. Digital capture reduces errors and deviations.

View term →

S

Statistical Process Control (SPC)

Use of data and control charts to monitor process variability and catch deviations before they generate non-conforming product. It increases stability and reduces waste.

View term →

P

Product Technical Data Sheet

Document containing a product's specifications: composition, physicochemical characteristics, allergens, storage conditions and shelf life. It's key for customers, audits and labeling.

View term →

F

FSSC 22000

GFSI-recognized certification scheme that combines ISO 22000, sector-specific PRPs and additional requirements. Widely adopted across the food manufacturing industry.

View term →

D

Document Management

Organizing, version control and access to quality documentation (procedures, records, certificates). Centralizing it digitally guarantees you always work with the current version and simplifies audits.

View term →

G

GFSI (Global Food Safety Initiative)

Initiative that recognizes and harmonizes the leading food safety certification schemes (IFS, BRCGS, FSSC 22000), making their acceptance by international retailers easier.

View term →

I

IFS Food

International Featured Standard. A GFSI-recognized certification standard that assesses the safety and quality of products and processes in food manufacturing, widely required by European retailers.

View term →

N

Non-Quality Indicators

Metrics on the cost of failure: rework, waste, claims, returns. Quantifying them turns quality into a financial argument and prioritizes improvement.

View term →

F

Food Safety

Assurance that a food product will not cause harm when consumed as intended. It relies on controlling biological, chemical and physical hazards across the entire manufacturing chain.

View term →

R

Receiving Inspection

Verification of raw materials and packaging on arrival against agreed specifications. Blocking non-conforming goods at intake prevents quality problems further down the line.

View term →

I

ISO 22000

International standard that integrates HACCP principles into a food safety management system (FSMS), applicable to any organization in the food chain.

View term →

P

Production KPI

Key indicator that measures factory performance: productivity, quality, waste, issues or OEE. Real-time KPIs enable agile operational decisions and continuous improvement.

View term →

P

Product Release

Quality decision that authorizes a batch to ship after verifying it meets all specifications and controls. A digital workflow speeds up release and leaves auditable evidence.

View term →

B

Batch Coding

Assigning a unique identifier to each group of manufactured product. It's the foundation of upstream and downstream traceability and shelf life control.

View term →

R

Raw Material

Input that enters the production process before transformation. Its receiving control, specifications and traceability determine the quality of the finished product.

View term →

C

Continuous Improvement (Kaizen)

Approach to continuously optimizing processes through small, sustained changes. It relies on plant data, KPIs and issue history to identify opportunities.

View term →

W

Waste

Losses of product or raw material during the process (breakage, scrap, rework, expired stock). Measuring and analyzing waste is one of the most direct levers to improve factory profitability.

View term →

M

MES (Manufacturing Execution System)

Software that controls plant operations in real time, connecting ERP planning with shop-floor execution. It complements the ERP by adding traceability and process control.

View term →

N

Non-Conformity

Failure to meet a quality, food safety or specification requirement, detected during production or inspection. Managing non-conformities systematically — recording, root cause analysis and corrective action — reduces rework, waste and customer claims.

View term →

O

OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness)

Metric that combines availability, performance and quality to quantify the real efficiency of a line or machine. It's the standard for detecting hidden losses in production.

View term →

C

Cleaning and Disinfection Plan

Documented hygiene program for facilities and equipment, with frequencies, products and owners. Verifying it is key to preventing contamination and passing audits.

View term →

P

Prerequisite Programs (PRPs)

Basic hygiene conditions and activities needed to maintain a safe environment (maintenance, pest control, training, cleaning and disinfection). They're the foundation the HACCP system is built on.

View term →

C

Critical Control Point (CCP)

Stage of the process where an essential control is applied to prevent, eliminate or reduce a food hazard to an acceptable level. Each CCP requires defined critical limits, continuous monitoring and documented records.

View term →

S

Supplier Claim

Formal communication of a non-conformity in raw materials, packaging or services (quality, quantity, deadlines or documentation). Managing claims with traceability protects margins and feeds objective supplier evaluation.

View term →

Q

Quality Record

Document or data point that provides evidence that controls and procedures were carried out on the plant floor. Digitizing records removes paper, speeds up audits and turns data into real-time indicators.

View term →

P

Product Recall

Recovery of product already distributed due to a safety risk or a specification non-conformity. Solid traceability enables fast, targeted recalls with less economic and reputational impact.

View term →

F

Food Safety

Set of practices that ensure food does not cause harm to the consumer. Covers hygiene, control of physical, chemical and biological hazards, traceability and regulatory compliance across the whole chain.

View term →

F

Food Traceability

Ability to follow a product's path across the whole chain, from raw material to the end consumer. It identifies the origin of each batch, enables fast recalls when an issue arises and meets a legal requirement (EC Regulation 178/2002) in the food manufacturing industry.

View term →

U

Upstream and Downstream Traceability

Ability to trace backward (raw materials and suppliers) and forward (product destination). Both are essential for targeted recalls and incident response.

View term →

P

Packaging Traceability

Control of the origin and batch of packaging materials (labels, films, containers). Relevant for allergens, recyclability and compliance with food-contact material regulations.

View term →

B

Batch Traceability

A batch groups units produced under uniform conditions under a single code. Its traceability is essential to locate product when an issue arises and to run targeted recalls instead of mass ones.

View term →

S

Shelf Life

Period during which a product keeps its quality and safety conditions. Determined through stability studies, it drives use-by dates and logistics.
